01 How to Update Your Latest Samsung OneUI 9.0 Device!#Sam-Update Samsung · Phone Settings · ~5 min

To update your Samsung Galaxy device to One UI 9.0, you can install the official stable version when it rolls out to your phone, or enroll in the active One UI 9.0 Beta Program if you own a supported flagship like the Galaxy S26, S25, or Z Fold series.

Option 1: Standard Official Update

  1. Connect your device to a stable Wi-Fi network and ensure your battery has at least 50% charge.
  2. Open the Settings app on your Galaxy phone.
  3. Scroll down and select Software update.
  4. Tap Download and install or Check for updates.
  5. If the One UI 9.0 package is ready, tap Update and follow the on-screen prompts. Your phone will automatically restart to finish installing.

Option 2: Install the One UI 9.0 Beta

If the stable version is not yet available for your device but your model is eligible, you can manually join the beta program via the Samsung Members App:

  1. Ensure your device has an active SIM card installed.
  2. Open the pre-installed Samsung Members app on your device.
  3. Swipe through the home banners or scroll to find the One UI 9 Beta Program invitation notice.
  4. Tap the banner, accept the terms and conditions, and tap Join.
  5. Wait 5 to 10 minutes for your enrollment registration to process.
  6. Navigate to Settings > Software update > Download and install.
  7. The Android 17-based One UI 9.0 beta update (typically a large download over 3.5 GB) will appear for installation.

Device Compatibility & What's New

  • Eligible Devices: The upgrade path is primarily rolling out to recent premium flagships, including the Galaxy S26, S25, S24 series, and newer foldables. Older models like the Galaxy S22 series, Z Fold 4, and older A-series mid-rangers are excluded from the roadmap.
  • Key Features: Upgrading to One UI 9.0 introduces Android 17 functionality alongside upgraded Galaxy AI tools. Highlights include the camera-tracking "My Fan Cam" video mode, an optimized "Call Brief" tool, and customized widget interfaces.
02 Create Windows 11 Bootable USB (Bypass TPM & Secure Boot) #usb-bootable Windows · Rufus · ~10 min

If you are installing Windows 11 on older hardware, Microsoft's strict hardware requirements (TPM 2.0, Secure Boot, and CPU generation limits) will block installation. You can bypass all checks using Rufus.

  1. Download the latest version of Rufus from rufus.ie.
  2. Download the official Windows 11 ISO directly from Microsoft's website.
  3. Plug in a USB flash drive (at least 8 GB). Warning: All data on this drive will be wiped.
  4. Open Rufus, select your USB drive under Device, and click SELECT to choose your Windows 11 ISO.
  5. Set Partition scheme to GPT and Target system to UEFI (non-CSM).
  6. Click START. A customization dialog will appear.
  7. Check "Remove requirement for 4GB+ RAM, Secure Boot and TPM 2.0". You can also check "Remove requirement for an online Microsoft account" to use a local account.
  8. Click OK and let Rufus write the image. Boot from the USB to install Windows 11 without hardware errors.
03 Top Hidden Samsung One UI Customization Tricks #oneui-tricks Samsung · One UI & Good Lock · ~5 min

Hidden customization tweaks and optimization settings for Galaxy phones and Foldables:

  • Remove Icon Labels (Clean Home Screen): Open Good Lock -> Home Up -> Home Screen -> toggle Hide App Icon Label to ON. Gives you an ultra-clean, minimal setup look.
  • Back Tap Shortcuts: In Good Lock -> RegiStar -> Back-Tap Action. Set double-tap or triple-tap on the back glass to take a screenshot, open the camera, or trigger flashlight.
  • Separate App Sound: Stream Spotify/YouTube Music strictly to a Bluetooth speaker while system notifications and TikTok stay on phone speakers. Settings > Sounds and vibration > Separate app sound.
  • Full Screen Multitasking: Open Good Lock -> MultiStar -> toggle Enable multi-window to all apps and Remove letterbox on foldables so apps utilize the full 7.6-inch inner display.
  • Deep Sleep Unused Apps: Prevent background bloatware from draining standby battery. Settings > Battery > Background usage limits > Put unused apps to sleep.
04 How to Fix Broken or Crashing Apps on One UI 9.0 #fix-apps Samsung · Troubleshooting · ~4 min

After upgrading to major Android 17 / One UI 9.0 builds, some older or un-optimized third-party apps may crash, freeze, or fail to launch. Follow this sequence to resolve app conflicts cleanly:

Step 1: Wipe Cache & Storage Partition

  1. Long-press the broken app's icon on your Home screen or App drawer and tap the (i) App Info button.
  2. Scroll down and tap Storage.
  3. Tap Clear cache in the bottom right corner (this removes corrupted temporary data without logging you out).
  4. If the app still crashes, tap Clear data (note: this resets the app back to initial install state).

Step 2: Disable Deep Sleep & Battery Restrictions

One UI 9 aggressive background management can prematurely kill active app services:

  1. In App Info, tap Battery.
  2. Switch the profile from Optimized to Unrestricted.
  3. Ensure the app is removed from Settings > Battery > Background usage limits > Deep sleeping apps.

Step 3: Run "Repair Apps" via Samsung Recovery Mode

If multiple apps force-close after the update, recompile Android runtime (ART) bytecode:

  1. Power off your Galaxy device completely.
  2. Connect your phone to a PC or Mac via USB-C cable.
  3. Press and hold Volume Up + Power until the Samsung logo appears to enter Android Recovery.
  4. Use the Volume Down key to highlight Repair apps and press the Power key to select it.
  5. Your phone will reboot and automatically re-index and optimize all installed APK packages.
05 How to Disable Auto Blocker & Sideload APKs on One UI #auto-blocker Samsung · Security & APKs · ~3 min

Samsung includes a security feature called Auto Blocker on modern One UI builds. While useful for preventing unauthorized USB commands, it completely restricts sideloading APK installation packages from sources outside the Play Store and Galaxy Store.

Step 1: Toggle Off Auto Blocker Sideloading Prevention

  1. Open the Settings app on your Galaxy device.
  2. Scroll down and tap Security and privacy.
  3. Select Auto Blocker.
  4. Toggle the main switch to Off (or disable Block apps from unauthorized sources).
  5. Authenticate using your biometric fingerprint or Lock Screen PIN when prompted.

Step 2: Grant Per-App "Install Unknown Apps" Permission

Android requires explicit permission for the browser or file manager handling the file:

  1. Navigate to Settings > Apps.
  2. Tap the three dots (top right) -> Special access -> Install unknown apps.
  3. Locate your browser (e.g., Chrome, Samsung Internet) or file browser (e.g., My Files).
  4. Toggle Allow from this source to ON.

Step 3: Bypass Google Play Protect Warning

If installing a custom mod, port, or developer tool, Play Protect may trigger a blocking dialog:

  • On the pop-up warning, tap More details.
  • Tap Install anyway to finish the package installation.
